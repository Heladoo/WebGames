# CLAUDE.md (drop-the-drop)

**Drop the Drop** (טיפות צבע לכוסות) is a calm game for young children, after a real activity: drops of coloured water on smooth paper are pushed with a stick into cups drawn on the paper. The player drags drops, joins drops of one colour into a bigger drop, keeps colours apart or mixes them, and takes each drop to the cup of its colour. Shared rules (Vercel, CSP, sound, saves, home screen) are in the root `CLAUDE.md`.

## Status

**Thirty-five levels** (`src/levels.ts`; numbers below are positions, ids stay `l1`…`l35` so saves keep working), slow and gentle:

- **1–2:** drag drops into a cup. Level 2's cup takes three small drops; dots on the glass count them.
- **3:** sort two colours.
- **4–5:** join drops, because the cup wants a bigger drop.
- **6–8:** join each colour and keep the colours apart. Level 6 is the first sample.
- **9–12:** mix colours (red + yellow = orange, and so on). Level 12 mixes one colour and keeps another apart.
- **13–14:** the in-between colours: turquoise (two blue, one yellow) and lime, with a blue cup that must keep one blue for itself.
- **15–20:** splitting. Level 15 teaches it (its hint comes after 2.5 s, `teach: 'split'`), then big drops are split to share one colour between two mixes. These levels use red-orange, indigo, magenta, lime and turquoise, and level 19 starts with a big green drop.
- **21: free mixing** (id `l29`, owner's request: children loved mixing): twelve tiny dots, four of each base colour, and a brown cup that wants one huge drop of all of them. Any join is fine, since the totals are equal; the level is `free`, so there are no mistakes and no dead ends.
- **22–24, 27–29:** glitter glue. Level 22 teaches it (`teach: 'glitter'`). From then on some cups want glitter and others must stay plain, so drops go through a stripe or around its end; levels 27–29 combine glue with splitting and the in-between colours.
- **25–26:** more sharing in the style of level 20 (big drops split between two or three mixes).
- **30–35: the paper towel** (owner's idea, after the real activity). 30–31 are free colouring: every drop soaks in, anywhere. 32 (id `l35`, `teach: 'towel'`) is one red dot, so the child learns to carry a drop to a target. 33–35 are pictures: a flower, a sun and three balloons (orange, green, purple; the owner didn't like the butterfly they replace). Each area wants its colour, mixed ones too (green leaves from yellow + blue, an orange sun), and a clear wrong colour shows the try-again card.

The first sample was approved. The owner's feedback since then is done:
- an in-scene celebration;
- the table background;
- choosing a level two ways (the next level, or the levels screen);
- landscape only;
- a more varied touch sound;
- names for every mixable colour, the plant growing from level to level, faster drops for fast fingers, a quicker celebration, the owner's logo;
- levels with in-between colours (turquoise and friends) and splitting a drop with a double tap;
- glitter glue stripes, more level-20-style levels, a stronger wiggle on touch, per-level stats; oil drops are designed in `BACKLOG.md`, not built yet;

Ideas still open are in `BACKLOG.md`.

## Principles (the owner asked for these)

- **The drops are the game.** The motion, joining, gloss and see-through look of the drops get the most care; everything else is simple.
- **Calm, but quick when the child is quick.** No timer, no score. A slow drag is capped at a calm `VMAX`, but when the finger runs far ahead the spring pulls harder and the cap rises to `VMAX_FAST` (owner's request). Touches are checked on every physics step, so a fast drop cannot jump over another one. Refusals are a gentle sway and two soft notes.
- **A mistake shows its reason, and the level can be replayed.** This is the owner's choice, an exception to the root "no fail state" rule. A level ends early only when a join makes a drop that no cup can take (`fitsACup`):
  - In levels that keep colours apart (`mixing: false`), touching another colour is that mistake. A drop of another colour trembles when you come near.
  - In mixing levels, it happens when a join holds more of a colour than any cup wants. For example, two blue drops when the green and purple cups each want one blue.
  - A drop that is too big is not a mistake when halving it would fit, since it can be split (`fitsACup` tries halves down to a volume of 1).
  - Either way the drops really mix on screen first. Then a card shows the sum as drops (`a + b = mix`), a sentence that explains it, and a big "again" button.
  - **A dead end** (`checkStuck`) also ends the level gently: when the solver (below) proves no way is left, a card shows the drops that are left and suggests trying again (after 1.2 s). Example from the owner: four green halves left in level 19, which can never make lime and turquoise.
- **Landscape only** (owner's decision): one fixed sheet of 160 × 100 world units (`WORLD_W`, `WORLD_H`), scaled to fit, with the table around it. On an upright screen, `#rotate` (CSS `orientation: portrait`) shows a phone turning, and the game ignores touches. `screen.orientation.lock('landscape')` is tried where the browser allows it. The manifest asks for landscape.
- **Hebrew UI, `dir="rtl"`, playable without reading:** buttons pair a word with an icon (`src/icons.ts`); a cup says what it wants with its colour, a dashed circle the size of the drop it takes, and dots when it takes several.

## How it works

- **Layout** (`game.ts`): the paper is `PAPER` (`render.ts`) inside the world. Cups sit in a row at the bottom, at `CupDef.x` or spread evenly. Drops start where the level puts them, and `spread()` pushes them apart, leaving more room between different colours.
- **Cups:** `need` is the size of drop a cup takes, `total` how much it holds (default `need`), `got` how much is in it. Cups can be any colour in `PIG`, including the in-between ones. A drop released over a cup goes in if its colour matches and its volume is at least `need` and at most the room left. Otherwise the cup throbs (wrong colour) or its dashed circle swells (too small), and the drop slides back to a free spot.
- **Soft-body drops** (`drop.ts`): a centre plus 64 radial offsets, each a damped spring toward a target shape, coupled to its neighbours (surface tension), with the area held at the volume (`keepArea`).
  - **Dragging:** the centre follows the finger on an under-damped spring (viscous lag, soft overshoot), and the target shape becomes a teardrop pointing at the finger. On release the target is round again and the springs ring down; that is the settle wobble.
  - **Joining** (`mergeInto`): the new outline starts as both old outlines seen from the joint centre (a peanut with a neck), and the springs pull it round. Pigment and volume add up.
  - **Touch** (`reach`): the dragged drop's outline reaching into another drop, so a stretched tip can touch first.
  - **Splitting** (`split`, owner's request): a double tap on a drop (a second tap on the same drop within 0.4 s, in the same place) splits it into two halves with half the paint each. The halves start overlapping and stretched toward each other, then slide apart in the direction with the most room (away from other drops, the edges and the cups) and round off. Drops smaller than 2 only wobble (`sfx.noSplit`).
  - **Wiggle:** a touch jiggles the drop: a squash toward the finger, a smaller three-lobed ripple and a little random shiver (`jiggle`). The outline damping `C` is light (5), so touches and releases ring a little longer (owner's request: "wiggle more when touched").
  - **Paint per volume:** one unit of paint per unit of water, so a level's big mixed drop (a green drop of volume 4) is `share(PIG.green) × 4`.
- **Glitter glue** (`Level.glue`, owner's request): stripes of glue on the paper, drawn into the backdrop as silvery glue with coloured flecks and a wet shine, plus a few glints twinkling every frame (`drawGlueTwinkle`).
  - **Picking up glitter:** a dragged drop whose centre comes within half the stripe's width (plus a little) gets `glitter` (`glueCheck`, every physics step). It jiggles, sparkles and shimmers (`sfx.glitter`). Glitter is kept through joins (either drop) and splits (both halves). Only dragged drops pick it up; drops that slide by themselves never do.
  - **Cups:** a cup with `glitter: true` has lots of glitter in its water and glitter all along its rim, with glints (owner's request: glitter, not a star). It takes only glittery drops.
  - **Glittery drops** show many flecks inside, a faint sheen and a few glints twinkling over them. Plain cups refuse glittery drops (the cup throbs).
  - **Mistakes:** a drop that gets glitter when no cup wants that colour with glitter ends the level with its own card. The card shows the sum as pictures (the drop + a heap of glitter, `drawGlitterPile`, = the drop with glitter) and says "…יש עכשיו נצנצים, וזה לא מתאים לאף כוס. נסו לעקוף את פס הדבק." So does a join that has glitter when only a plain cup could take it.
  - **Hints:** they know about glue. A pour or join that must stay plain goes round the stripe's free end (`around`, `via`), and a drop that a glitter cup would take is led into the nearest point of the glue. Level tiles ring a glitter cup's dot in gold.
- **The paper towel** (`src/towel.ts`, `Level.towel`). Only a target for now; the owner may add it as an obstacle later.
  - **Simulation:** a grid of cells (0.8 world units) with water and three dyes. Water flows from wetter to drier cells, with a random resistance per cell (the fibres), and carries its dye. Dye also blends between wet neighbours (`MIXD`), so overlapping stains really mix instead of pushing each other aside. Everything dries slowly (`DRY`); dry dye stays put. It only runs while something is wet.
  - **Pictures are cut out of towel** (`cut`): only the pieces are towel, with bare paper between them, and nothing flows from piece to piece (paint spreads within its piece only).
  - **Drawing:** the stain is a grid-sized picture, smoothed 3× and then stretched by the browser over the towel, on its own layer (`#towel`, between `#bg` and `#stage`). Each cell's amount is averaged with its neighbours for soft edges. A darker rim marks where dye gathered at the edge (the "coffee ring"), and wet cells are darker.
  - **Soaking on touch** (owner's expectation, like real paper towel): the moment a dragged drop's outline touches towel (`towelContact`), the drag ends and the drop soaks in at that spot (`soak`, `Drop.soakAt`), into the piece it touched, shrinking while the towel takes its water and dye. The towel is in the still scene: a soft white quilted sheet for free colouring; for pictures, cut-out pieces with a little shadow, a pale hint of each area's colour, and decorations on the paper (the balloons' strings, `DECOR`).
  - **Free colouring** (`free`, no picture): done when every drop has soaked in.
  - **Pictures** (`PICTURES`): areas made of shapes. Each shape is a piece unless shapes share a `piece` (a stem with its leaves). `need` is the paint an area takes, in drops, matched to its pieces (one drop per petal or ray). Judged by eye (`areaLook`):
    - **Colour per cell:** a cell counts as coloured only where it shows the area's colour, so yellow and blue side by side don't make a green area; they have to meet and mix.
    - **Done:** every piece is at least half coloured, or the area was given all its paint and every piece is at least a quarter coloured.
    - **Mistake:** more than a fifth of the area is clearly a colour it doesn't use (over half of the cell's dye, like red in the leaves), once the paint has settled. Small bleeds are fine. The card shows the colour it got ≠ the colour it wants: "בעלי הכותרת יש עכשיו …, אבל צריך שם …".
    - **Dead end:** an area still lacks a colour that no drop left has.
  - **Hints** (`suggestTowel`): for each area, a drop that already has its colour goes to the middle of its least-coloured piece (`areaCentre`). For a mixed colour, the two drops that make it are joined first. Since a drop soaks into the first piece it touches, the path (`planPath`) must touch no other piece and no other drop: it tries straight, out of the column of drops through a gap (`outOfTheRow`), a spot beside the target, and walks round the outside of the whole picture (`Towel.bounds`), and takes the shortest clear one. Its margin is bigger ahead of the drop than beside it, since a dragged drop stretches toward the finger. Pieces are spaced so even a drop of volume 2 fits between them (the sun's rays at radius 24, scale 1.1). Hints can have several turns (`via` is a list).
  - **Layout:** picture levels have the picture on the right (`PIECES`) and the drops on a 3 × 3 grid on the left (`SLOTS`, `at()`), with room to leave between them.
- **Colour** (`color.ts`): drops carry red, yellow and blue pigment amounts; the colour comes from an RYB cube (paint-like mixing). `sameColor` compares proportions.
  - **Names:** every colour a child can mix has a Hebrew name (`colorName`, `COLOR_HE`). These are the three base colours, the three secondary ones, and the in-between ones: אדום-כתום, צהוב-כתום, ירוק-ליים, טורקיז (blue + green), כחול-סגול, סגול-אדום, and חום. A colour without an everyday adjective reads "טיפה בצבע טורקיז" (`aDrop` in `main.ts`).
- **Rendering** (`render.ts`, Canvas 2D):
  - **Drops:** a shadow and a coloured light pool (soft blobs painted once, stretched like the drop), a see-through body whose gradient stretches with the drop, a dark inner rim, a caustic on the far side, a reflected rim line and a window highlight on the light side (top left). Big drops are flatter: the rim width is capped, so their middle is clearer and their gloss smaller.
  - **Backdrop** (`drawBackdrop`): light oak planks, the black diamond card from the reference photo (its corners peek out above and below the paper), the paper sheet, and a plant whose leaves lean over the paper's top-left corner. It is painted once per resize and per level.
  - **The plant grows over the levels** (`LEAVES`, `plant()`), up to the last ones: each leaf has the level it appears at (the last at 31). New leaves sprout small and light green and grow over the next levels, and every leaf turns a little from level to level. A pink bud appears at level 9 and blooms by level 11, a violet one appears at level 15 and blooms by level 17, and a yellow one appears at 25 and blooms by 29 (`flower()`).
  - **The plant's corner stays free:** no drop starts under the plant or the top-left buttons (`PLANT` in `game.ts`; `spread()` moves such drops down).
  - **Cups:** marker drawings with scalloped water.
  - **Speed on old tablets** (owner's report: the celebration and busy levels stuttered). Measured in Chromium with the CPU slowed 6× (`Emulation.setCPUThrottlingRate`), about 140 ms a frame went to 20–35 ms. What did it:
    - **The still scene on its own canvas** (`#bg`, under `#stage`): it is painted only when the level or the size changes. The game canvas is cleared and draws only what lives. Copying a full-screen picture every frame was the biggest cost.
    - **Adaptive resolution** (`frame`): when frames stay slower than about 30 ms for 2 s, the resolution drops (2 → 1.5 → 1) for the rest of the visit; it is capped at 2.
    - **Resting drops:** a drop that has stopped is drawn from a cached picture (`drawDropCached`, `Drop.still`) and sleeps in the physics until touched. Glittery ones refresh a few times a second. This needed a fix in `keepArea`: its target is now the polygon's own area, and the uniform part of the outline's speed is removed. Before, every drop drifted forever and never counted as still.
    - **No live blurs:** drop shadows and light pools are soft blobs painted once per colour (`softBlob`). The stickers, the "כל הכבוד!" writing, the celebration droplets and the button-splash droplets are pictures painted once (`sprite`, `dropImage`).
    - **Physics:** at most 8 steps per frame, and only drops near the dragged one are checked for touches.
    - **Sound, without losing any of it** (the owner did not want a thinner sound). The celebration's sound is *recorded*: when a level starts, `prepareParty(cups)` mixes the same recipes (all seven bells, the eight-saw pad, the sticker pops and bells, the cup hops, and the 2.2 s room) into an `AudioBuffer` in an `OfflineAudioContext`, in the background. The recipes come from the same functions, with `rendering` and `shift` set. At the celebration `playParty` plays that one buffer; if it isn't ready yet, the sound plays live as before. Timings come from `PARTY` (stars, `hop0`, `hopGap`, `hopLand`), so the recording and the pictures stay in step. The slide noise plays only during a drag, which changes nothing you can hear.
- **Celebration** (`updateParty` / `drawParty`, timing in `PARTY`, quick on the owner's request; the buttons come after about a second): when the last cup fills, the cups hop one by one with little fountains, three star stickers pop onto the paper, and "כל הכבוד!" is written in marker from right to left in the cups' colours. Then the buttons (next, again, home) float over the paper (`#win`, no card).
- **Choosing a level, two ways** (owner's request; `main.ts`):
  - **The next level:** Play/Continue on the home screen resumes a level under way, or, after a finished level, opens the next one. The "next" button at the end of a level does the same.
  - **The levels screen** (`#pick`): opened from the home screen's "Levels" button or the "Levels" button at the end of a level. It shows a tile for every open level (its cups' colours, and a star when done), plus the next one, locked. A level opens when the one before it is done. The house button goes back to the home screen. Leaving a level under way asks first.
- **Buttons are liquid jelly** (owner's request, to match the logo; the "liquid buttons" block in `styles.css`): a glossy gradient body with a darker rim, a white shine and a slightly uneven blob shape; a press squashes it. (A small drop hanging from the main buttons was removed: the owner found it detached and confusing.) Blue is the main way on, pale aqua the others, green "yes", and orange the big "next". The top-bar buttons are bubbles and the level tiles are jelly squares.
- **Buttons splash** (`src/splash.ts`, owner's request, as the transition between screens): pressing a menu button, a level tile, or the top bar's home or restart button squashes it, bursts it into droplets of its own colour (drawn on the `#splash` canvas over everything) with a splashy sound, and runs the button's own action 0.23 s later. A capture-phase click listener holds the click and clicks the button again afterwards, so the buttons' handlers did not change. The sound toggle stays instant. With reduced motion there are fewer droplets and no delay.
- **Home:** the owner's logo (`public/logo.webp`, 600 px; its size follows the screen height), Play/Continue and Levels, then the Dorothy Games logo. On tall screens the logo is on top; on phones held sideways (560 px tall or less) it is on the left with the menu on the right, so the card never scrolls (owner's request). The menu buttons share one width, with their icons in one column and their words starting at one line (owner's request). The owner removed the "from the start" button and the subtitle.
- **The solver** (`src/solver.ts`): after every change (start, join, split, glitter, pour; not every frame), `think()` searches the moves a player can make with the drops as they are, breadth first. The moves are join two, split one, give one glitter, and pour one into a cup. Amounts are kept in quarters, so halves of halves stay exact. The answer is `yes` with the first move of a shortest solution, `no` (a dead end), or `unknown` (more than 12,000 states; then the game assumes it can be done). It is pruned by `couldFit`, which drops any state where a drop could never end up in a cup. Every level's start takes under 50 ms (level 27 is the biggest). It replaced rules of thumb that missed dead ends and hinted joins before the needed split (owner's report).
- **Hints:** after 7 quiet seconds (2.5 s in a teaching level), a see-through fingertip shows the solver's first move (`suggest()`): a pour, a join (the smaller drop moves to the bigger), a split (the finger taps twice, `tap2`), or a drop led into the glue. Paths go round glue when the drop must stay plain. If the solver gave up, the old rules of thumb (`suggestRough`) are used.
- **Sound** (`sfx.ts`):
  - **Touching a drop:** never the same twice. The pitch comes from the drop's size and colour and walks a pentatonic scale on quick touches. Each touch also gets a random pan, a random envelope, sometimes a soft fifth, and a short noise "tick".
  - **Dragging:** a quiet pink-noise slide sound that follows the speed. Letting go gives a low settle plop.
  - **Splits:** two falling plops, a little apart and panned left and right, with a soft bell.
  - **Joins:** a plop and a bell, with two more bells when colours mix. Each cup gets a note (a chord when full).
  - **Celebration:** hop plops, sticker pops and the win arpeggio.
  - All of it goes through a room reverb and a compressor.
- **Save** (`state.ts`): `drop-the-drop:v1` keeps the mute setting and the finished level ids.
- **Anonymous stats** (the shared pattern from the root `CLAUDE.md`, prefix `dd:`): `src/stats.ts` → `api/stats.js` → Upstash, with the dashboard at `public/stats.html`. A play is counted once per visit, when the first level starts. Each level start and each finished level is counted per level id (`HINCRBY dd:levels`, `dd:won`; one command each), and the dashboard shows a table of levels started and finished. Play time ticks only while a level is on screen (not paused, not upright, not hidden), with a report every 2 minutes and a beacon on page hide. Nothing is counted on localhost or `?debug` URLs. The home screen's small line says what is counted.

## Debug hooks

- `?debug=state` exposes the game as `window.__dd` (`snapshot()` gives positions in CSS pixels); the tests use it. `?level=N` (1–35) picks the level (by position).
- `?debug=play` skips the home screen. `?debug=party&level=N` plays that level's celebration at once. `?debug=lab` is a sandbox with three drops of each base colour, mixing on. `?debug=sheet` shows every colour at three sizes.
- The icons (`public/icon-*.png`, `favicon.png`, `apple-touch-icon.png`) are the owner's logo, resized; so are `logo.webp` and the portal's `art/logos/drop-the-drop.webp`. `?debug=icon` still shows one big drop.
- `og-image.png` is a 1200 × 630 screenshot of the home screen.
